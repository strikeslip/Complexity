## The beginning of the "paradigm shift" in AI development

In the field of AI, especially reinforcement learning (RL), humans have been designing learning algorithms with their brains. Reinforcement learning is a mechanism in which agents (AI) learn behavioral policies (policies) that maximize "rewards" through trial and error while interacting with the environment. Just as a small child learns how to ride a bicycle while falling, AI also learns the way to success from a huge number of failures.

The learning algorithm defines this "learning method" itself. What information should we focus on, how we can use past experience, and how we deal with future uncertainty? These rules have been gradually improved by genius researchers around the world for many years based on mathematical insights and rules of thumb. However, this approach essentially had the limitations of "human intuition and knowledge."

The breakthrough in this study is that it fundamentally overturns this premise. Instead of letting AI solve specific tasks, the researchers asked, "What are the most efficient learning rules?" He asked a more basic question. And he found the answer to his own. Just as humans can find the best "study method" through trial and error, AI has also developed the "ultimate learning method" through its own experience. This approach is called "Meta-Learning", or "Learning how to learn", and it is attracting attention as a way of opening up a new horizon for AI research.

## How was the discoRL born? Meta-learning inspired by evolution

So how did AI discover learning rules that transcend human design? The process is a magnificent, computational natural selection story reminiscent of Darwin's theory of evolution.

### Magnificent Darwinic selection by parent AI "meta-network"

The research team built not a single genius AI. They first generated a "digital population" consisting of a huge number of AI agents with a wide variety of learning rules. These agents are deployed in various virtual environments with complex rules (such as many video games), each attempting to solve a given task.

This is the core of this study. The parent AI called the "Meta Network" oversees their struggles. This metanetwork coolly evaluates how fast each agent has been able to learn and how well they have been able to learn. Based on that evaluation, they fine-tune the learning rules used by the next generation of agents to "evolve" them into better ones.

This process is truly a simulation of biological evolution. Individual agents are "individuals", learning rules are "genes", and metanetworks are "natural selection mechanisms" that select excellent genes, mating and mutating, and passing on to the next generation. By repeating this cycle for generations, only the rules of inefficient learning are eliminated, and only those rules that are highly adaptable to the environment, that is, those with extremely high learning efficiency, survive.

A technology called "gradient-based meta-optimization" was the driving force behind this evolution. This is differentiating all of the vast experiences the agent has accumulated throughout its life and calculates which parts of the learning rules should be modified in which direction to maximize overall learning efficiency. This allows the meta-network to find a path to "better learning rules" extremely efficiently, rather than just random trial and error.

### Discovering "unknown learning rules" that go beyond human intuition

At the end of this evolutionary approach, a new learning rule, DiscoRL (Discovering Reinforcement Learning), was born. DiscoRL had a unique structure that was different from any algorithm that humans had ever designed. It may be said that it is an algorithm designed by "alien intelligence" that was born as a result of purely performance pursuit, free from restrictions such as cognitive biases of human researchers and mathematical ease of use.

People tend to think about things to the extent that they can understand, or as an extension of existing theories. However, there are no such preconceptions in the automatic discovery process by AI. Only the vast amount of data from millions of agents, billions of trial and error, tells the only truth. As a result, a new principle of learning was discovered that man had never thought of, or that was too complex to give up on formulation.

## The power of discoRL to defeat the "kings" designed by humans

To prove that the newly discovered DiscoRL is not only unique, but also powerful, the team faced a direct confrontation with the strongest algorithms already designed by humans.

### Overwhelming victory in Atari benchmark

The classic Atari 2600 video game group is widely used as a "standard test" to measure the performance of the reinforcement learning algorithm. These simple-looking games are also a very difficult task for AI to understand the situation from a list of pixels and continue to choose the best course of action in real time.

The team tested an AI agent with DisoRL on a benchmark consisting of 57 Atari games (from this evaluation, DiscoRL is also called "Disco57") that consists of 57 Atari games. The comparisons were "PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization)" which has led the research of enhanced learning in recent years, and "MuZero", which is the successor to AlphaGo, which defeated the world champion in Go, and "MuZero" that mastered chess and shogi without being taught rules, such as algo-rhysms that should be called champions.

The result was amazing. DiscoRL suppressed the rivalry these humans had designed and scored the highest scores across the entire benchmark. This is a symbolic event that shows that the automatic discovery by AI has finally outstripped the accumulation of knowledge and experience of human experts.

### Challenging unknown challenges – proof of versatility

Just by scoring high on a specific test, you can't say that you have really good learning ability. What is important is how flexibly you can be when faced a completely new unknown challenge that you have never experienced in the process of learning, that is, "glority".

To test this, the team took an even more rigorous test of the DiscoRL. It is a more complex and unpredictable game group with procedural (automatically generated environments each time you play), such as ProcGen, Crafter, and NetHack. These are benchmarks that can never be captured by mere pattern memorization, and true adaptability is required.

Here, too, the DiscoRL performed at the state-of-the-art level. This is eloquently demonstrated that DiscoRL has not simply learned the "chip strategy" specialized in Atari games, but has acquired a more universal and powerful "learning principle" itself that can be applied to diverse environments. In response to the skepticism that the environment of the game is simple and different from the real world, DiscoRL is a form that imposes a powerful refutation with its own performance.

## What this discovery means – The future where AI will create AI

The birth of DiscoRL is not just an academic achievement. It is full of serious suggestions that are deeply related to the future of AI and, in turn, the future of humanity itself.

### Is it a realistic step towards "recursive self-improvement"?

A concept that has been talked about for many years among AI researchers is "Recursive Self-Improvement." This is hypothesized that the cycle of AI improving its intelligence and making it smarter will improve its own intelligence more efficiently and more efficiently, leading to an exponentially increase in intelligence and lead to an "Intelligence Explosion" that goes far beyond human intelligence.

The results are nothing more than the core part of this cycle, i.e., the AI itself has improved its own "method of becoming smarter" (learning algorithm). Of course, this alone does not mean that there will be an immediate intelligence explosion. However, the process that was previously only a theoretical possibility, "AI will design the basis of AI", is the meaning that has been demonstrated as a real technology is immeasurable. It is a small but critical step towards the grand goal of recursive self-improvement.

### The end of the human "algorithm designer" era?

However, there is one question that arises. Does this mean that the role of humans designing AI algorithms themselves will be lost? The answer is "yes and no."

Certainly, the task of manually fine-tuning individual parameters and delving into mathematical formulas, which has previously occupied a large part of AI research, may be gradually entrusted to AI. From this point of view, the "age of manual craftsmanship algorithms" has shown signs of ending.

However, what is even more essential is the fact that new and important responsibilities have been born. The role of humans will change from becoming the "hand" of AI, to becoming a "compassionjo" that shows the direction to go.

Top runners such as Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hasavis and David Silver, the paper's senior author, have long talked about this vision of "automating scientific discoveries with AI." ## The Google DeepMind team, including David Silver as a co-author, chose the 57 Atari 2600 games (from the Arcade Learning Environment, or ALE, benchmark) because it is one of the most extensively studied and challenging benchmarks in reinforcement learning (RL) history.

These 57 games—often called Atari-57—require agents to master complex strategies, planning, long-term credit assignment, and adaptation across diverse tasks, making them a rigorous testbed for RL algorithms.

Hundreds of RL methods (e.g., DQN, PPO, MuZero) have been evaluated on this exact set over the past decade, providing a standardized way to compare performance metrics like interquartile mean (IQM) scores.

In the paper "Discovering state-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithms" (published October 22, 2025, in *Nature*), they meta-trained their discovered RL rule—**DiscoRL (short for "Discovered Reinforcement Learning")**—specifically on these 57 games to create **Disco57**.

The paper explicitly states: "To see how strong the rule can be when discovered directly from this benchmark, we meta-trained an RL rule, Disco57, and evaluated it on the same 57 games."

This in-distribution training (128 agents cycling through the games on 1,024 TPUv3 cores for 64 hours) let Disco57 achieve a new state-of-the-art IQM of 13.86, surpassing baselines like MuZero and DreamerV3 at 200 million steps per game.

They also trained a broader **Disco103** on 103 environments (including Atari-57, ProcGen, and DM Lab-30) to show generalization to unseen benchmarks like Crafter and Sokoban.

The number 57 isn't arbitrary—it's the fixed suite from ALE, popularized by DeepMind's own prior work (e.g., Agent57 in 2020, the first to beat human baselines on *all* 57 games).

No other count was needed; using fewer would limit comparability, while Atari-57 balances diversity, difficulty, and computational feasibility for large-scale meta-learning.

## DiscoRL vs. Music AI

"DiscoRL" is a Google DeepMind project related to automating the discovery of reinforcement learning algorithms (a form of AI architecture design) and is not a music-specific tool itself. The "RL" stands for Reinforcement Learning, not "Rhythm and Lyrics" or anything musical. 

Google DeepMind has developed several AI models for music generation, but they are separate from the DiscoRL project. 

### DeepMind Music AI Projects

DeepMind's music-focused projects are part of a broader effort to use AI for creative purposes, often in collaboration with YouTube and artists. 

**MusicRL**: This model is built on the earlier MusicLM model and uses reinforcement learning from human preferences (RLHF) to enhance the audio quality and text adherence of the generated music. This model applies the principles of reinforcement learning (the "RL" part) to music generation, which may be the source of confusion with "DiscoRL".

**Lyria**: This is DeepMind's most advanced AI music generation model to date, which powers experiments like "Dream Track" on YouTube Shorts. It excels at generating high-quality instrumentals and vocals and offers nuanced control over style and performance.

**Music AI Sandbox & MusicFX**: These are sets of experimental, user-facing tools that allow musicians and creators to generate new instrumental ideas, craft vocal arrangements, and break through creative blocks using models like Lyria and LIA 2.

**V2A**: This technology generates full soundscapes, including music and sound effects, that accompany video content by combining video pixels with natural language prompts. 

While DiscoRL is an interesting development in AI research, the related projects focused on music are MusicRL, Lyria, and the various tools in the Music AI Sandbox.
